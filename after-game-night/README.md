# After Game Night

A cozy first-person tidying game made in Godot 4. Game night is over. Six board games are still set out mid-play around a living room lit by a crackling fire, candles and lamps. Pack each game away properly, then carry each box to its spot on the game shelf.

## The collection

There are thirteen game lines across two shelves: 34 boxes holding about 9,200 pieces and 6,900 cards, roughly 16,100 things to sort. The room has a main play area and a hobby nook at the front with a hobby desk, a felt poker table, a card table, a crystal curio table and a marble ring. Real big-box games set the starting point (*Slay the Spire: The Board Game* ships with 650+ cards and 113 tokens; a Warhammer 40,000 starter set has about 40 miniatures), and then everything is tripled. Some of every game has also spilled onto the floor beside its table.

### The dice

The board-game d6 is a rounded-edge cube with drilled, painted pips (light pips on dark dice, dark pips on pale ones), laid out like a real die so opposite faces add up to 7.

The polyhedral dice are real polyhedra: tetrahedron, cube, octahedron, pentagonal trapezohedron, dodecahedron and icosahedron. Every face has a printed numeral in a slab font, and opposite faces add up the way they do on real dice. The 6 and 9 are underlined, the d10 runs 0–9, the percentile die reads 00–90, and the d4 has three numbers per face, one at each corner, so the corner pointing up gives the roll. Numerals always contrast with the die. Each finish has its own material:

| Finish | Look |
| --- | --- |
| Ember | glowing orange with glowing gold numerals |
| Frost | glassy ice blue with a bright rim glint |
| Galaxy | deep purple with star specks and glowing silver numerals |
| Jade | polished green with gold numerals |
| Bone | matte ivory with dark numerals (Wyrmlight Tales) |
| Obsidian | glossy black with glowing red numerals (Monster Vault) |

Each line was played with its expansions mixed in, so the pieces from every box in a line are jumbled together on one table. You can tell them apart by color: base game pieces are the line's own color, the first expansion's are darker with gold accents, and the second expansion's are lighter with cream accents. Card sets have their own colored stripe on the back.

## Packing a box

- **Scoop:** click a piece to scoop up a handful of the same kind nearby. The pieces you'd get are outlined before you click.
- **Sweep:** hold the button and drag across a pile to gather everything under the crosshair, up to 80 pieces.
- **Sift:** handfuls can be mixed. Pour one into a box and it takes only its own pieces; the rest stay in your hand for the next box. The prompt tells you how many of your handful belong in the box you're aiming at.
- **Pour:** pieces hop over the rim and drop into their spots one after another, each with a woody click, rising notes and little glints.
- **Card piles:** pick up one pile, then click more piles to carry up to eight at once. They sift into boxes the same way.

Nothing floats. The layout plans heights with a coarse height map, then a settling pass sets every piece, card pile, board and rulebook onto what's really beneath it. It casts rays at the centre and corners of each footprint, lowest things first, and repeats until nothing hovers. The autotest reports the result (0 floating over 2 mm). Everything laid out stays put until you pick it up, so nothing slides off a table into a box by itself.

`-- --tables <dir>` renders a close-up of every game.

### Seeing your sorting

- **Sorted!** When the last piece or card pile lands, the camera floats up over the box and slowly drifts around it.
  - Every compartment and card tray lights up in a wave across the insert, with a rising run of notes and a warm glow.
  - A banner shows how many pieces and cards are in place, and you earn Tidy Tokens.
  - Click to skip. Then the board and rulebook go on top and the lid closes.
- **Peek:** look at any packed box that isn't shelved yet and press F. The lid swings open and the board and rulebook lift out of the way so you can see the insert again. Press F again to close it.
- **Your collection:** when everything's shelved, choose *See your collection* on the end screen.
  - Each box comes down to a lit display stand, opened up, with its piece, card and compartment counts.
  - Browse with the arrow keys or the buttons, drag to turn the view, and press Esc when you're done.
  - Every box goes back on the shelf as it was.

### Boxes and cases

Board games come in traditional boxes: a deep lid that slides over the base, with the whole lid printed as one image. The illustrated lid art and the game's logo are rendered together into the lid, so the title is part of the print and doesn't sit on top. The front edge is printed the same way. Each game has its own display typeface, a backing plate (ribbon, cartouche, plaque, hanging sign or honeycomb badge), a drop shadow, a thick outline and a bevel highlight; some titles arch. Collections get proper cases instead: a brass-cornered dice vault with an engraved brass plate, an aluminum chip case with a stamped plate, a glossy deck vault with a printed logo strip, a glass-topped marble display case with a brass plaque, and a leather-strapped paint chest with a stencilled name. Rulebook covers and the shelf labels are printed the same way.

The logo fonts are open-source (SIL Open Font License) and bundled in `fonts/`, with their licenses in `fonts/licenses/`: Cinzel Decorative, Lobster, Audiowide, Alfa Slab One, Bungee, Rye, MedievalSharp and Righteous. Logos are styled in `scripts/logo_kit.gd`; the cases are built in `scripts/case_kit.gd`.

### Cards you can see

Cards are 1.65× life size and pieces 1.1×, so you can read them from standing height. Packed pieces keep up to 90% of that size in their boxes (only the fullest boxes shrink them more to fit), so a finished insert is easy to look over. Every card set has its own printed fronts on matte card stock: a frame in the set's colour, a named title bar, an illustrated art window, a type line, rules text and a cost gem. Casino decks have real playing-card faces, holofoils shimmer over their art and neon cards glow. On the tables, hands are fanned face up, some piles are dealt out in face-up rows, and most draw piles show their top card, so you can see what's what just by looking around. In the box, each set has a tray with a divider and tab in the set's colour, and the stack shows its top card face up. Faces are drawn in `scripts/face_kit.gd`.

### Miniatures

The miniatures are sculpted from capsule limbs on bevelled slate bases with grass and tufts:
- an armoured knight with a great helm, plume, sword and heater shield
- a hunched goblin with big ears and a spiked club
- a robed hero with a hood and a glowing staff
- a skeleton with ribs, a round shield and a spear
- a winged dragon with horns, spines and a curling tail

### Physics

Physics runs at 120 ticks per second with extra solver iterations and a tight penetration allowance. When a piece settles, it is snapped onto the surface below, and anything dropped is kept above the floor, so nothing sinks into tables or falls through the ground. The autotest drops pieces from head height and checks this.

### Tidy Tokens and the Tool Chest

You earn Tidy Tokens (✦) as you sort:

- **+1** for every compartment you fill
- **+3 or more** for every box you pack (bigger boxes pay more)
- **+8** for every game line that's fully shelved

Press **Tab** to open the Tool Chest and spend them. Every upgrade has levels, so there's always something to save up for. Tools you've bought go on your belt; switch with 1 to 4 or the mouse wheel.

| Upgrade | Levels |
| --- | --- |
| Seeker Lantern (✦3, 8, 16) | Aim at a box and fireflies mark everything of it still out; aim at a piece and a beam shows its box. Fireflies then linger longer, then stay until it's all put away. |
| Sorting Caddy (✦5, 12, 22) | Carry 140, then 220, then 320 pieces, with scoops 1.5×, 2× and 3× bigger. |
| Box Beacons (✦8, 14) | While you're holding pieces, every box that wants some of them glows with a count. Level 2 shows them through walls and furniture. |
| Dustpan (✦10, 18, 30) | Sweep up loose pieces of any kind, up to 150, then 250, then 400, in a wider circle each time. |
| Magnet (✦14, 22, 34) | Pull every matching piece within 2 m, then 4 m, then across the whole room. |
| Quick Pour (✦4, 9, 16) | Pieces pour into boxes 1.5×, 2× and 3× faster. |
| Card Satchel (✦6, 14) | Carry 16, then 30, card piles at once. |
| Sorting Chute (✦40, 60) | When you pour, leftovers fly on to their own open boxes within 4 m, then anywhere in the room. |

A full game earns roughly 400 tokens, and buying everything costs about 365, so you'll own most of the chest by the end. The upgrade list lives in `scripts/upgrades.gd`, and `_apply_upgrades()` in `scripts/main.gd` sets what each level does.

Rewards along the way: a full compartment chimes and sparkles, a packed box closes with a thunk and a puff of light, a streak counter climbs while you sort without mix-ups, and the room tidiness meter at the top celebrates every 10%.

Every box has a fitted insert with a compartment for each piece type. Flat things like coins and tiles stack in their compartments. Faint outlines show where everything goes. Packing order works like the real thing:

1. Pieces and cards first. Card piles go in the tray for their set.
2. Then the board (base games only), folded in half over the insert.
3. The rulebook goes in last. Then the lid closes.

All the boxes in a line share a footprint, so they stack together in that line's cubby on the shelf. Putting something in the wrong box counts as a mix-up. Getting the order wrong doesn't; you just get a reminder.

## How it works

The game pieces, cards, boxes, logos and sounds are all made in code when the game starts. The house is dressed with real furniture, decor and photo-scanned materials from [Poly Haven](https://polyhaven.com) (CC0, public domain). They live in `assets/`, with a list in `assets/CREDITS.md`. Godot imports them the first time you open the project. `scripts/props.gd` loads and sizes the models, and `TexKit.pbr()` in `scripts/tex_kit.gd` builds the materials. The game tables are stretched so their tops sit exactly where each game is laid out.

- `scripts/main.gd` builds the room, deals out the games and runs the game flow.
- `scripts/player.gd` handles first-person movement, picking up, carrying, placing and the tools.
- `scripts/pieces.gd` manages all the small pieces. Resting pieces are drawn in batches with lightweight colliders for aiming; only pieces in flight get full physics. This keeps 1,300+ pieces running smoothly.
- `scripts/item.gd` is for the bigger things you carry one at a time: card piles, boards, rulebooks and boxes.
- `scripts/game_box.gd` is a box with a fitted insert. It sizes its compartments, stacking depth and height to fit its own contents, accepts things in packing order, then closes its lid.
- `scripts/shelf_slot.gd` is a shelf cubby where all of one line's boxes stack.
- `scripts/mesh_kit.gd` builds all the pieces, plus the cards, boards, rulebooks and textures.
- `scripts/game_data.gd` lists every game line, its boxes, their pieces and cards, and how they're left on the table.
- `scripts/sfx.gd` synthesizes the sounds: wooden clacks, notes and a cardboard thunk.
- `scripts/hud.gd` draws the on-screen text and the title, pause and end screens.

Each new game shuffles where pieces sit, how they're piled, and the order of the shelf. About one piece in twelve ends up on another table or the couch.

### Tweaking

`VOLUME` at the top of `scripts/main.gd` multiplies every piece and card count (3.0 by default). `SPILL`, `STRAY_CHANCE` and `PIECE_SCALE` control how much ends up on the floor, how much wanders to other tables, and how big pieces are; `CARD_SCALE` sets card size. Boxes and their inserts grow to fit (`IW` and `MAX_ID` in `scripts/game_box.gd`), and `CUBBY_H` sets shelf cubby height. The autotest prints every shelf stack and flags any that is too tall. Edit `LINES` in `scripts/game_data.gd` to change what's in each box; the inserts and boxes resize themselves. `HANDFUL` sets how many of each piece one click scoops up.

The lighting is designed for Godot's default Forward+ renderer, which adds the warm haze and soft bounce light.

### Testing without the editor

```
godot --headless --path . -- --autotest
```

This checks that handfuls only scoop matching pieces, that the dustpan, magnet and lantern find what they should, that buying upgrades applies them, that Box Beacons light the right boxes and that the Sorting Chute sends every piece home, that a mixed handful poured into a box gives up exactly that box's pieces, that expansion pieces, an early board and wrong shelf cubbies are refused, then packs and shelves all 34 boxes and confirms the game ends.
